ALASKA DEPARTMENT OF SNOW (ADS) - ROAD CLOSURE AND REOPENING PROCEDURES
Procedure number: ADS-OPS-150 | Revision 3 | Effective 1 October 2026
Owner: Director of Operations | Fictional document created for training purposes.

1. CLOSURE CRITERIA
A road segment is closed when ANY of the following applies:
  - Visibility is below 1/4 mile due to blowing snow for more than 30 minutes.
  - Avalanche danger is rated HIGH or EXTREME on the segment, or an avalanche has reached the road.
  - Ice or drifting makes the segment impassable for a plow with a wing blade.
  - A crash or stranded vehicles block all lanes and State Troopers request a closure.
  - Scheduled avalanche control work (closures of up to 2 hours, announced 24 hours ahead when possible).

2. WHO CAN CLOSE A ROAD
  - P1 routes: the Storm Duty Officer, or Alaska State Troopers for life-safety emergencies.
  - P2 to P4 routes: the Regional Operations Supervisor.
  - Every closure must have a closure ID, start time, reason, and an estimated reopening time.

3. NOTIFICATION
  - Post the closure to 511 traveler information within 15 minutes.
  - Notify State Troopers dispatch, affected municipalities, and the Alaska Railroad if the
    closure is within 1 mile of a rail crossing.
  - Update the estimated reopening time at least every 2 hours.

4. REOPENING CHECKLIST
  1) The closure condition has ended (visibility, avalanche danger reduced to CONSIDERABLE or lower,
     debris cleared).
  2) At least one full plow pass and treatment in each direction.
  3) A field supervisor has driven the segment and approved reopening.
  4) Post the reopening to 511 within 15 minutes.

5. PILOT CARS AND CONVOYS
  - When a segment is passable but hazardous, ADS may run pilot-car convoys instead of a full
    closure. Convoys leave every 30 minutes and are limited to 25 vehicles.
  - Vehicles without adequate tires (studded or winter-rated) may be turned back.

6. PUBLIC GUIDANCE DURING CLOSURES
  - ADS staff never advise drivers to use unmaintained bypass roads.
  - Questions about specific trips should be referred to 511 (dial 511 or visit 511.alaska.example).
