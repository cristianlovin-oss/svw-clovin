ALASKA DEPARTMENT OF SNOW (ADS) - AVALANCHE SAFETY PROCEDURES
Procedure number: ADS-SAF-420 | Revision 5 | Effective 1 October 2026
Owner: Avalanche Program Manager | Fictional document created for training purposes.

1. AVALANCHE-EXPOSED ROUTES
ADS monitors avalanche paths on these segments:
  - Seward Highway, Milepost 75 to 90 (Turnagain Pass and Bird to Girdwood).
  - Richardson Highway, Thompson Pass (Milepost 18 to 37).
  - Glenn Highway near Sheep Mountain (Milepost 110 to 115).
  - Egan Drive approaches in Juneau below Mount Juneau (seasonal monitoring).

2. DANGER RATINGS
ADS uses the North American Public Avalanche Danger Scale (Low, Moderate, Considerable, High, Extreme)
as published by the regional avalanche center.
  - CONSIDERABLE: no stopping in marked avalanche zones; crews work in the zone only with a spotter.
  - HIGH: the segment is closed (see ADS-OPS-150) and avalanche control is scheduled.
  - EXTREME: the segment is closed and no ADS personnel enter the runout zones.

3. AVALANCHE CONTROL
  - Control work is carried out only by certified ADS avalanche technicians.
  - Explosives handling follows ADS-SAF-425; no other staff may handle explosives.
  - Control missions require a closure of both directions, typically up to 2 hours.

4. CREW SAFETY
  - Every crew member working in an avalanche zone carries a transceiver, probe and shovel.
  - Plows do not stop in marked avalanche zones when the danger is CONSIDERABLE or higher.
  - If a crew member is caught in an avalanche, call 911 and the Storm Duty Officer immediately.

5. PUBLIC INFORMATION
  - ADS does not give backcountry travel advice. The public should be referred to the regional
    avalanche center forecast and to 511 for road status.
